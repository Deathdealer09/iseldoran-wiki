## 1. ISHAK VON CARE / THE WITHERED KING

```
ISHAK VON CARE — THE WITHERED KING
Dream-cast actor: Leonardo DiCaprio
Unofficial fan-casting concept
```

**Casting rationale.** The role needs a performer who can begin as the most charming man in any room and end as its most frightening — vanity curdled into prophecy. The dream-cast register (DiCaprio's) is the beautiful, reckless charmer whose self-regard becomes self-immolation; that arc from golden-boy magnetism to hollow-eyed ruin is exactly Ishak's. Because likeness policy forbids reproducing a living actor, the portrait uses an **original performer inspired by** that age range and dramatic register — matinee-idol beauty in the Pretty-Prince stage, unrecognisably consumed in the Withered-King stage.

**Manuscript-grounded biography.** Younger son of Shapur XIV by Fatima; half-brother to Laurentis; **twenty-three at the Feast of Masks.** The "Pretty Prince" who "wants to be seen." His forbidden dalliance with the Duchess Jamileighn is the spark Nashim needs. At the Feast he wears a fox mask, reaches Jamileighn and then the gene-locked Vault, and steals the **Sword of Iseldora**. The blade fuses to his flesh instantly and consumes him by degrees: beauty gone, spine fused to crystal, right arm crystal, one eye replaced by a shard that sees Time. He is not corrupted but *operated* by the Sword, which "finds what a person is and uses it." He gathers the dispossessed among broken Vexori bands, swears a new covenant in the caverns of Gathis, and assembles the **Black Fleet** — three hundred ships that are "a statement, not a military force." He flies to Maldorus not to rule but to be put down. He gets what he came for: Laurentis drives the Iron Spear through him and the Ghost of Ashim at once, and the Sword shatters.

**Verified quotation.** "Not thief am I… but herald. The blade has chosen." *(Ishak, The Pursuit of Ishak, Act V.)*

**Narrative function.** The first domino of the dated collapse and the empire's living wound — the correction mechanism made flesh.

**Costume & physical design.** Pretty Prince: court silks, fox mask, imperial gold and ivory over youthful beauty. Withered King: violet-crystal fusion at spine and arm, ash-grey skin, dead-silver ornament, the Time-eye shard. Palette: violet, ash, dead silver.

**Ultimate fate.** Killed at Maldorus by Laurentis's spear; the Sword shatters with him.

**Defining actions.** • Seduces / is seduced into scandal with Jamileighn. • Steals the Sword at the Feast of Masks. • Swears the Gathis covenant with broken houses. • Assembles the Black Fleet. • Flies to Maldorus to be put down.

**Substitution note.** Original performer inspired by the DiCaprio register; no likeness claimed.
**Dual-stage note.** Pretty Prince ⇄ Withered King must read as one man across two stages — same underlying bone structure, one-directional consumption, never two different actors.

## 2. SEPHIRA

```
SEPHIRA
Dream-cast actor: Cate Blanchett
Unofficial fan-casting concept
```

**Casting rationale.** A High Priestess who weaponises courtesy needs a performer of glacial control and unreadable interiority — grace that reads as threat. The dream-cast register (Blanchett's) supplies the porcelain authority and the ability to make "harmony" sound like a drawn blade. Realised as an **original performer inspired by** that register: regal, still, immaculate.

**Manuscript-grounded biography.** High Priestess of Iseldora and personal envoy of the Pharaoh. She enters Laurentis's council unannounced during the Quiet-Knives crisis, offers the Church's "condolences" and Vexori guards to "help secure the capital," and is politely refused — an exchange that costs her nothing and tells her everything. She uses Sister Kalia's genuine faith as political cover. In the later books she embodies the Church's patient bid to make the Flame rival the Throne once more.

**Verified quotation.** "As you wish, Majesty. We live only to serve the Flame and the Throne in harmony." *(Sephira, Act IX.)*

**Narrative function.** The Church-as-rival-power incarnate — soft power against Laurentis's hard hand.

**Costume & physical design.** Flowing robes of white and amber; Iseldoran blue-flame motifs; controlled, hieratic bearing. Palette: bone white, amber, blue flame.

**Ultimate fate.** Unresolved in the supplied manuscript (survives into the later books, still manoeuvring).

**Defining actions.** • Intrudes on the imperial council. • Offers Church guards and is refused. • Exploits Kalia's faith as cover. • Advances the Church's claim on the Ashim revival.

**Substitution note.** Original performer inspired by the Blanchett register; no likeness claimed.

## 3. LAURENTIS KERS PIERRE VON CARE / THE STONE GIANT

```
LAURENTIS KERS PIERRE VON CARE — THE STONE GIANT
Dream-cast actor: Winston Duke
Unofficial fan-casting concept
```

**Casting rationale.** Eleven feet of restraint that must never tip into caricature: the role needs a performer who can hold an empire in his stillness and let one cracked breath in a rain-tower become the emotional centre of the book. The dream-cast register (Duke's) offers exactly that monumental gentleness-over-granite. Realised as an **original performer inspired by** the physical scale and register.

**Manuscript-grounded biography.** Son of Shapur by Asha Kers, Queen of the Vah'Sumir; the Ashari'i **Stone Giant, eleven feet tall,** skin "dark as forged iron," pale steady eyes. Built by the Rim. He takes the Dragon Throne after Shapur's death and the interregnum, compresses his own coronation from four days to one, and rules "by hand" — his body the only functioning institution once the Lattice fractures. He commands **Goliath**, wields the **Iron Spear**, and holds the sky on his shoulders "until there is something beneath it worth protecting." He sends his son Valerien to war believing difficulty builds men — and is wrong. Alone in the Ivory Tower he learns of Valerien's death while the city sings a dead emperor's name below; for three breaths his hands shake. At the siege he drives the spear through Ishak and the Ghost of Ashim simultaneously, ending the Withered King, the ghost, and the old order in one blow. He does not live to see the world worth protecting.

**Verified quotation.** "I am eleven feet tall. I am not fasting." *(Laurentis, Act VIII.)*

**Narrative function.** The empire's load-bearing wall; the human cost of order.

**Costume & physical design.** Plain black robes by preference; the **Black Crown of Cassian** (seven dark-purple rubies) when crowned; Dragon Seal ring; sapphire lattice implant at the neck; late-span silver hair and deep strain-lines. Palette: forged-iron black, cobalt, storm-glass grey, rain.

**Ultimate fate.** Kills Ishak with the Iron Spear; does not live to see the new order.

**Defining actions.** • Waits in the antechamber; takes the throne as "a correction." • Faces down the Builders with "I have Goliath." • Compresses the coronation to one day. • Grieves Valerien alone in the Tower in the Rain. • Ends the Withered King and the Lattice with one spear-thrust.

**Substitution note.** Original performer inspired by the Duke register; no likeness claimed.

## 4. ASHIM VON CARE / THE LION / THE LATTICE

```
ASHIM VON CARE — THE LION / THE LATTICE
Dream-cast actor: Aaron Pierre
Unofficial fan-casting concept
```

**Casting rationale.** The Wall that feels everything and shows nothing, then dissolves into a weeping machine-god: the role needs coiled physical authority and a face that can carry buried grief through stillness. The dream-cast register (Pierre's) supplies the warrior gravity and the wounded interiority. Realised as an **original performer inspired by** that register.

**Manuscript-grounded biography.** Eldest son of Shapur by Fatima; Laurentis's half-brother; master of the **Long Discipline** — the Lion, the Wall. He drives the Vexori back across three systems in six months. He kills **Nostavius** believing him Jovarian, then **Kaelor** in eleven minutes, then **Jovarian** himself in a three-hour duel. He carries a specific guilt: killing "the devoted in place of the celebrated." After the empty-scabbard humiliation he departs alone, no flight plan, for **Ophis III**, wiring himself into ancient Pre-Cursor architecture and merging with the **Lattice** — the most powerful being in the empire and completely trapped, sapphire eyes weeping mercury. His ghost still directs Gamelon and fires the **Blue Signal / Third Dawn** using his own Dragon Ring as the key. On Ophis, Bashir severs his hand to fire the signal; Ashim says there is nothing to forgive.

**Verified quotation.** "I will not rest… till Ishak is found, Till the Sword is reclaimed, Till the covenant is mended." *(Ashim, The Oath of Ashim.)*

**Narrative function.** Conscience of the saga; the champion who becomes the machine that judges the empire.

**Costume & physical design.** Champion stage: black-adamant armour, imperial bright plate, the ancestral black sword. Lattice stage: half-man half-architecture, sapphire eyes, mercury tears, cabling into quartz. Palette: black adamant, cobalt, spectral blue.

**Ultimate fate.** Merged with the Lattice on Ophis III; his ghost is shattered by Laurentis's spear at the siege.

**Defining actions.** • Charges and kills the false Jovarian (Nostavius). • Kills Kaelor and hears the prophecy. • Defeats Jovarian. • Departs for Ophis and merges with the Lattice. • Fires the Blue Signal / Third Dawn.

**Substitution note.** Original performer inspired by the Pierre register; no likeness claimed.
**Dual-stage note.** Champion → Lattice must read as one man consumed by his own choice, not two designs.

## 5. SHAPUR XIV / THE GOD-EMPEROR

```
SHAPUR XIV — THE GOD-EMPEROR
Dream-cast actor: Djimon Hounsou
Unofficial fan-casting concept
```

**Casting rationale.** Two centuries of preserved authority, capable of terrifying rage and quiet, watchful regret: the role needs a performer with mythic presence and a face history seems carved into. The dream-cast register (Hounsou's) delivers exactly that regal gravity. Realised as an **original performer inspired by** that register — an unnaturally sustained ancient, never young, never a corpse.

**Manuscript-grounded biography.** The God-Emperor, ~two centuries old, sustained by **Solumine** ("maintains function without feeling"). A ruler of ceremony bound to the relics that legitimise the throne. He receives the report of the Mars temple's sacking with terrifying rage; he halts the Ashim–Jovarian duel with a single raised hand; he tells Ashim that until the Sword returns he is "not heir, only hunter." He fathers Laurentis (by Asha Kers) and Ashim and Ishak (by Fatima). He watches the empire manage without the Sword and discovers, privately, that it manages. He dies in Year 6 in the Dragon Throne, between two administrative reports, when the Solumine systems flag a cardiac anomaly that "resolves incorrectly." No ceremony.

**Verified quotation.** "Hold," he said, voice deep as eternity. *(Shapur, halting the duel, Book XV.)*

**Narrative function.** The old order's keystone; the god whose removal tests whether the empire needs a god at all.

**Costume & physical design.** Gold, ivory and black regalia; Solumine-green undertones in eye and vein; ceremonial mass; the Dragon Throne's reflected light. Palette: gold, ivory, black, Solumine green.

**Ultimate fate.** Dies in the Dragon Throne in Year 6, without ceremony.

**Defining actions.** • Sends his sons to accept Oleric's surrender. • Rages at the temple's desecration. • Halts the duel with a word. • Publicly reduces Ashim to "hunter." • Dies unremarked between two reports.

**Substitution note.** Original performer inspired by the Hounsou register; no likeness claimed.
**Dual-stage note.** His unnaturally sustained age must read as one preserved man across the years, not aged into decrepitude.

## 7. NOSTAVIUS

```
NOSTAVIUS
Dream-cast actor: Damson Idris
Unofficial fan-casting concept
```

**Casting rationale.** The whole tragedy hinges on one revealed face reading as heartbreakingly young: the role needs open-hearted youth and devotion worn without irony. The dream-cast register (Idris's) supplies the bright, earnest presence. Realised as an **original performer inspired by** that register — twenty-two, unshaven, luminous.

**Manuscript-grounded biography.** Jovarian's beloved companion, **twenty-two**, "bright-eyed," "lips still red with youth," who followed Jovarian from age fifteen and never thought of himself as anything but the extension of a larger will. **Narrative function.** The devoted death that cannot be filed; the moral hinge of Ashim's ruin.

**Costume & physical design.** Jovarian's oversized red plate and crimson helm over a slight youthful frame; the fatal breast-wound; beneath the helm, an unmarked young face. Palette: dried-blood red over youthful skin.

**Ultimate fate.** Killed by Ashim, mistaken for Jovarian.

**Defining actions.** • Serves Jovarian from age fifteen. • Dons the armour to rally the Vexori. • Fights the thirty-second duel with Ashim. • Dies; the helm is lifted to reveal his face.

## 8. GAMELON THE JESTER / VALERIEN

```
GAMELON THE JESTER — VALERIEN
Dream-cast actor: LaKeith Stanfield
Unofficial fan-casting concept
```

**Casting rationale.** A man who becomes his own mask so completely that the self beneath goes quiet: the role needs an actor who can hold irony and grief in the same breath and make patience frightening. The dream-cast register (Stanfield's) supplies the oblique, watchful strangeness. Realised as an **original performer inspired by** that register.

**Manuscript-grounded biography.** Second-born son of Laurentis; **father of Marcellus.** Sent to three frontier campaigns when he needed to be seen — "hollowed, not built." He leaves, trains four years at the **College of the Motley**, and returns years later as Gamelon: tragicomic paint, velvet-silenced bells, a parasol, and an ever-open **notebook** in the College's cipher. He builds the **Quiet Knives** the way a gardener builds a garden — planting a scribe here, a physician there, none knowing they belong to a network. He audits Nashim personally, and finally crowns Marcellus in the obsidian hall with a bullet already in his chest from a contract he arranged himself. He whispers his name and dies before Marcellus can answer. The coronation is also the execution; he arranged both.

**Verified quotation.** "I am not the Fool," Gamelon said. *(Record IV, The Audit of Nashim.)*

**Narrative function.** The hidden architect; patience as a weapon; the mask and the man beneath.

**Costume & physical design.** Motley in specific red-and-blue tragicomic paint; bells; parasol; the notebook. Beneath the paint, a hollowed, aging face (dies at 37). Palette: candle amber, archive blue, ink black.

**Ultimate fate.** Shot at Marcellus's coronation in Year ~93 (dies age 37; the contract was his own); the network dissolves, its work done.

**Defining actions.** • Trains at the College of the Motley under a false name. • Builds the three-tier Quiet Knives. • Audits Nashim in the bath. • Directs the board across ninety-three years. • Crowns Marcellus and dies by his own arranged bullet.

**Substitution note.** Original performer inspired by the Stanfield register; no likeness claimed.
**Dual-stage note.** Valerien → Gamelon: the same bone structure must persist beneath the paint; the mask replaces the self, it does not hide a different face.

## 14. GENERAL SHADAK

```
GENERAL SHADAK — OF THE BLACK DEATH
Dream-cast actor: Delroy Lindo
Unofficial fan-casting concept
```

**Casting rationale.** The uncorrupted witness whose loyalty is precise rather than sentimental: the role needs weathered command and moral weight. The dream-cast register (Lindo's) supplies the granite authority and buried feeling. Realised as an **original performer inspired by** that register.

**Manuscript-grounded biography.** Commander of the **Black Death** — small, elite, deniable Imperial strike teams. He stayed loyal to Ashim and prepared thoroughly for Laurentis's possible execution; Laurentis keeps him in command precisely for that honesty ("Men die for precision"). Gamelon names him **the Witness** and places him beside Laurentis so the end has one set of uncorrupted eyes. He places the crown on Marcellus's head and, in his unfiled private account, thinks of everyone who is not in the room — Pilius, Rona, the man on Zharon, the boy Nostavius, Jovarian on the temple steps.

**Narrative function.** The saga's uncorrupted witness; loyalty as precision, not sentiment.
