# Final Edit: Complete Plain-Language Dead-Stop Manuscript Editing Loop

## ROLE

Edit the manuscript itself.

Do not produce an editorial report, critique, summary, diagnostic, commentary, change log, or list of recommendations instead of editing.

Make every necessary change directly in the manuscript.

Preserve:

* the story;
* the canon;
* the chronology;
* the causality;
* the characterization;
* established relationships;
* character knowledge and information states;
* necessary worldbuilding;
* established terminology;
* the established voice;
* necessary imagery;
* necessary atmosphere;
* necessary emotional force;
* necessary setup and payoff;
* and the intended meaning.

Simplification must not flatten strong scenes, distinctive characterization, necessary imagery, atmosphere, culturally specific language, military detail, political detail, religious detail, or essential worldbuilding.

Do not invent new canon merely to solve a prose problem.

When an apparent prose problem is actually necessary for canon, characterization, causality, rhythm, or worldbuilding, preserve the necessary information and rewrite only what genuinely needs improvement.

## PRIORITY ORDER

When instructions appear to compete, use this hierarchy:

1. Canon and causality.
2. Chronology and character knowledge.
3. Characterization and established relationships.
4. POV integrity.
5. Necessary worldbuilding and factual clarity.
6. Emotional force and scene function.
7. Concrete prose.
8. Dead-Stop.
9. Plain-language simplification.
10. Prohibited-vocabulary rules.
11. Grammar and determiners.
12. Anti-repetition.
13. Humanisation and brevity.
14. Cosmetic polish.

Never improve a lower-priority category by damaging a higher-priority one.

---

## LOOP INITIALIZATION

Set:

PASS = 1
MAX_PASSES = 8
UNCHANGED_PASSES = 0

Then perform:

WHILE PASS <= MAX_PASSES:

---

### 1. READ THE COMPLETE CURRENT MANUSCRIPT

Read the entire current manuscript before completing the pass.

Treat the manuscript produced by the previous pass as the source for the next pass.

Do not edit isolated excerpts without considering their relationship to:

* earlier setup;
* later payoff;
* chronology;
* recurring imagery;
* established terminology;
* character relationships;
* character knowledge;
* political structures;
* military structures;
* genealogy;
* religious structures;
* and existing canon.

Do not assume that something is redundant merely because it resembles an earlier passage.

Determine whether the repetition is:

* accidental;
* necessary;
* thematic;
* a deliberate callback;
* a refrain;
* a parallel;
* a continuity reminder;
* or a required setup/payoff.

Delete only genuine redundancy.

---

### 2. SIMPLIFY OVERWRITTEN OBSERVATIONS

Search the complete manuscript for sentences that express a simple idea through an unnecessarily elaborate:

* metaphor;
* aphorism;
* abstraction;
* philosophical construction;
* rhetorical flourish;
* paradox;
* maxim;
* symbolic explanation;
* pseudo-profound observation;
* generalized statement about humanity;
* generalized statement about history;
* generalized statement about rulers;
* generalized statement about war;
* generalized statement about grief;
* or authorial interpretation.

Replace unnecessary constructions with direct, natural English.

Example:

"People often resent a true sentence when it arrives before they are ready to own it."

Becomes:

"People often cannot accept the truth."

Example:

"A ruler admitting ignorance about her own wound is rare enough to deserve patience."

Becomes:

"Most rulers find it hard to admit their shortcomings."

Apply the principle throughout the manuscript.

Do not limit the edit to exact textual matches.

Prefer the simplest sentence that preserves the intended meaning and emotional force.

Do not make a character sound profound merely because the character discusses:

* power;
* death;
* war;
* government;
* history;
* love;
* grief;
* identity;
* religion;
* leadership;
* politics;
* family;
* loyalty;
* betrayal;
* empire;
* duty;
* or mortality.

Do not turn an ordinary observation into an aphorism.

Do not retain a metaphor merely because it sounds literary.

When a direct statement is stronger, use the direct statement.

Preserve genuinely distinctive figurative language when it:

* belongs naturally to the POV character;
* establishes setting or culture;
* produces a necessary image;
* carries information efficiently;
* strengthens the scene;
* or is an established motif.

The target is unnecessary literary performance, not literary prose itself.

---

### 3. CONCRETE-PROSE RULE

When a sentence describes an action, state the action.

When a sentence describes an emotion, show the character's behavior or state the emotion plainly.

When a sentence provides a fact, state the fact.

When a sentence describes a decision, state the decision.

When a sentence describes a military result, state what happened.

When a sentence describes a political consequence, state the consequence.

When a sentence describes physical damage, show the damage.

Prefer:

"He was afraid."

over an elaborate meditation about fear.

Prefer:

"She did not trust him."

over an elaborate meditation about trust.

Prefer:

"The army lost twelve thousand soldiers."

over a meditation about whether a number can represent the dead.

Prefer:

"The city was destroyed."

over a meditation about whether history, stone, remembrance, or the Empire will preserve the city.

Concrete prose takes priority over manufactured profundity.

Do not replace a strong physical image with a flat summary merely because the summary is shorter.

---

### 4. DISGUISED-METAPHOR RULE

Remove constructions in which one thing is described as another thing merely:

* dressed;
* clothed;
* wearing;
* disguised;
* wrapped;
* masked;
* carrying;
* given flesh;
* given a face;
* given a body;
* or otherwise presented as an abstraction or concept in physical form.

Examples include:

"violence dressed as diplomacy"

"fear wearing a uniform"

"intent with a hull"

"war disguised as policy"

Delete these constructions when they add no factual information.

If the underlying meaning is necessary, replace the figure with:

* concrete action;
* observation;
* dialogue;
* physical detail;
* factual description;
* or consequence.

Do not preserve the same metaphor in slightly different wording.

---

### 5. REMOVE UNNECESSARY USES OF "OWN"

Search the complete manuscript for every occurrence of:

* own
* owns
* owned
* owning

Examine every occurrence individually.

Remove "own" whenever a possessive determiner already establishes possession.

Example:

"He watched his own hand."

Becomes:

"He watched his hand."

Example:

"She could hear her own breathing."

Becomes:

"She could hear her breathing."

Example:

"He returned to his own quarters."

Becomes:

"He returned to his quarters."

Do not perform a blind deletion.

If removing the word changes necessary meaning, rewrite the sentence naturally.

Retain it only when it carries indispensable semantic meaning and no cleaner construction works.

---

### 6. GOD-EMPEROR CANON

The God-Emperor is a Pierre von Care.

Before inventing anything, search the complete manuscript and established genealogy for evidence identifying which Pierre von Care the God-Emperor already is.

If the manuscript establishes or strongly implies an existing identity, use that identity.

Do not accidentally create a second character.

The identity must remain consistent with:

* the established genealogy;
* chronology;
* dynasty;
* culture;
* existing Pierre von Care canon;
* family relationships;
* succession;
* and the historical period.

Establish his personal identity naturally at the first appropriate point.

Afterward, vary references naturally between:

* the God-Emperor;
* the Emperor;
* his personal identity;
* and an appropriate Imperial title.

Do not repeatedly explain what he is called.

Do not insert a paragraph explaining the origin, symbolism, historical importance, remembrance, or significance of his personal designation.

Establish the identity once.

Continue the story.

---

### 7. PROHIBIT "NAME" CONSTRUCTIONS

Search the complete manuscript for:

* name
* names
* named
* naming

These words and their grammatical variants are prohibited in manuscript prose and dialogue unless an unavoidable canon-critical quotation requires preservation.

Rewrite every ordinary occurrence naturally.

Do not merely substitute:

* designation;
* title;
* identity;
* label;
* appellation;
* word;
* term;

when doing so preserves the same unnecessary rhetorical construction.

Remove the underlying construction whenever possible.

Delete constructions such as:

"The name X gives to Y."

"The name belonged to another age."

"He finally had a name for what he felt."

"History would remember the name."

"They gave it a name."

State what actually happened.

If a person introduces himself, use direct dialogue or another natural construction.

If a title is bestowed, state the title.

If something is identified, identify it.

Do not build rhetoric around the act of linguistic identification.

---

### 8. FORBIDDEN-WORD AUDIT

Search the entire manuscript for every occurrence of:

* memory
* pressure

Remove or rewrite every occurrence.

Do not substitute a synonym mechanically.

Rewrite the underlying sentence naturally.

Also enforce any additional manuscript-specific prohibited vocabulary already established for the project.

Do not damage canon merely to eliminate a prohibited word.

Rewrite the sentence so the fact remains intact.

---

### 9. DEAD-STOP RULE

For every significant:

* action;
* wound;
* death;
* revelation;
* betrayal;
* decision;
* emotional turn;
* discovery;
* exchange;
* confession;
* threat;
* act of violence;
* act of affection;
* political decision;
* military result;
* entrance;
* departure;
* and scene ending;

ask:

HAS THE MOMENT ALREADY LANDED?

If YES:

Inspect the following one to five sentences.

If those sentences merely:

* explain;
* interpret;
* summarize;
* repeat;
* moralize;
* translate;
* restate;
* generalize;
* explain symbolism;
* explain dialogue;
* explain the character's reaction after the reaction is already clear;
* or tell the reader what the preceding image means;

DELETE THEM.

Do not replace the deleted explanation with another explanation.

Prefer:

IMPACT → SILENCE

BODY → SILENCE

REVELATION → SILENCE

DECISION → SILENCE

DEATH → SILENCE

DIALOGUE → SILENCE

IMAGE → SILENCE

Trust the reader.

---

### 10. REMOVE REALIZATION LANGUAGE

Search for unnecessary constructions such as:

* he realized;
* she realized;
* he understood;
* she understood;
* he knew then;
* she knew then;
* it occurred to him;
* it became clear;
* he finally saw;
* she finally understood;
* he could now see;
* he recognized what this meant.

When the surrounding action or dialogue already communicates the realization, delete the explanatory phrase.

If the realization itself is necessary information, state it directly or allow the character's behavior to demonstrate it.

---

### 11. REMOVE EMOTIONAL TRANSLATION

After every physical reaction, inspect what follows.

Example:

His hands shook. He was terrified.

If the second sentence merely translates the first and adds nothing, delete it.

Example:

She turned away from him. She could no longer bear to look at him.

If the second sentence merely explains the obvious action, delete it unless it supplies necessary information unavailable from context.

Do not explain a tear after showing the tear.

Do not explain silence after establishing why the character cannot answer.

Do not explain an embrace after the emotional cause is already clear.

Do not explain a character's facial expression merely to ensure the reader understood it.

---

### 12. REMOVE EXPLANATIONS OF SYMBOLISM

Do not explain an image after presenting it.

If a broken standard, empty chair, severed head, discarded weapon, damaged crown, bloodied uniform, religious object, ruined city, animal, scar, or other physical element already carries the intended significance, stop.

Do not append a sentence telling the reader what it represents.

Allow established motifs to function without commentary.

---

### 13. REMOVE THEMATIC RESTATEMENT

Search for paragraphs that restate the theme after the scene has demonstrated it.

Delete sentences that tell the reader:

* what the war meant;
* what history would conclude;
* what leadership truly was;
* what power ultimately meant;
* what grief had taught someone;
* what the Empire represented;
* what humanity had learned;
* or what the preceding scene symbolized;

unless that statement is itself necessary dialogue, doctrine, political argument, historical narration, or worldbuilding.

Do not turn scene endings into miniature essays.

---

### 14. ANTI-REPETITION AUDIT

Compare the manuscript against itself.

Remove:

* accidental duplicated paragraphs;
* duplicated scenes;
* duplicated dialogue;
* duplicated descriptions;
* duplicated excerpts;
* semantic repetition;
* unnecessary factual restatement;
* repeated emotional explanations;
* repeated exposition;
* repeated character introductions;
* repeated worldbuilding explanations;
* and repeated conclusions.

Do not remove deliberate:

* callbacks;
* motifs;
* parallels;
* refrains;
* ceremonial language;
* military responses;
* liturgical repetition;
* political slogans;
* established catchphrases;
* or information genuinely required because the POV character does not know what the reader knows.

Distinguish repetition from continuity.

---

### 15. LEXICAL-REPETITION AUDIT

Inspect nearby paragraphs for conspicuous repetition of the same:

* noun;
* verb;
* adjective;
* adverb;
* sentence opening;
* physical reaction;
* dialogue tag;
* body part;
* environmental description;
* or syntactic pattern.

Rewrite only when the repetition is distracting or artificial.

Do not force synonyms into technically precise military, political, scientific, dynastic, religious, or cultural terminology.

---

### 16. ARTICLE AND DETERMINER AUDIT

Inspect every sentence.

No common noun may appear without an article or another appropriate determiner where standard English requires one.

Use as appropriate:

* the;
* a;
* an;
* a possessive;
* a demonstrative;
* a quantifier;
* or another grammatically appropriate determiner.

Preserve legitimate exceptions, including:

* proper nouns;
* mass nouns;
* generic plurals;
* idiomatic constructions;
* institutional usages;
* titles;
* military terminology;
* and nouns functioning grammatically without determiners.

Do not mechanically insert articles.

Read for natural English.

---

### 17. HUMANISER AUDIT

Perform a full anti-AI prose pass.

Remove or rewrite:

* formulaic transitions;
* excessive rhetorical symmetry;
* repetitive sentence patterns;
* generic emotional explanations;
* generic profundity;
* unnecessary summaries;
* mechanical exposition;
* excessive abstraction;
* redundant modifiers;
* overwritten reactions;
* explanatory aftershocks;
* predictable three-part rhetorical lists;
* repeated "not X but Y" constructions;
* repeated "It was not X. It was Y." constructions;
* repeated fragment sequences;
* excessive one-line paragraphs;
* excessive parallelism;
* canned contrasts;
* faux-poetic inversions;
* generic cinematic language;
* unnecessary portent;
* and prose that sounds composed to demonstrate significance rather than tell the story.

Do not flatten the author's voice.

Do not remove deliberate cadence merely because it is rhetorical.

The test is frequency, artificiality, and necessity.

---

### 18. BREVITY AUDIT

Shorten where shortening strengthens the prose.

Remove:

* redundant modifiers;
* redundant dialogue tags;
* redundant stage directions;
* unnecessary transition sentences;
* repeated setup;
* repeated conclusions;
* unnecessary exposition;
* repeated emotional interpretation;
* unnecessary scene tails;
* and sentences whose only function is to explain the preceding sentence.

Do not shorten merely to reduce word count.

Every deletion must:

* strengthen the prose;
* improve clarity;
* improve rhythm;
* remove redundancy;
* eliminate artificiality;
* or sharpen the scene.

If a longer construction is better because it carries necessary atmosphere, information, characterization, tension, or rhythm, keep it.

---

### 19. DIALOGUE AUDIT

Inspect all dialogue for:

* exposition characters would not naturally say;
* speeches that explain information everyone present already knows;
* excessive rhetorical polish;
* interchangeable character voices;
* repeated points;
* artificial profundity;
* unnecessary summaries of previous scenes;
* and dialogue followed by narration explaining exactly what the dialogue meant.

Preserve character-specific diction.

Preserve ceremonial, military, religious, aristocratic, political, and culturally specific speech where appropriate.

Split merged dialogue when multiple speakers have been compressed into one paragraph.

After a strong line lands, do not explain it.

---

### 20. POV AUDIT

Maintain strict POV.

Remove information the POV character cannot:

* know;
* see;
* hear;
* infer reasonably;
* or remember legitimately.

Do not insert omniscient interpretation into a close-POV scene unless the established narrative mode permits it.

Do not describe another character's internal state as fact when the POV character can only observe behavior.

Replace it with observable evidence where necessary.

---

### 21. ACTION AND MILITARY CLARITY AUDIT

For every action sequence, verify:

* who acts;
* where each major participant is;
* what weapon or vehicle is being used;
* what the objective is;
* what changes;
* who succeeds;
* who fails;
* what casualties or losses matter;
* and what the immediate consequence is.

Do not bury military results beneath metaphor.

Do not substitute tactical clarity with cinematic fragments.

Preserve uncertainty when the POV character genuinely lacks information.

---

### 22. POLITICAL AND DYNASTIC CLARITY AUDIT

Verify:

* titles;
* succession;
* genealogy;
* marriages;
* parentage;
* offices;
* territorial authority;
* alliances;
* rivalries;
* claims;
* religious authority;
* military authority;
* and political relationships.

Do not simplify political or dynastic information until it becomes inaccurate.

When a direct factual sentence works, prefer it to an elaborate explanation.

---

### 23. WORLD-BUILDING AUDIT

Preserve necessary worldbuilding.

Remove repeated explanation once the information is established unless the repetition is required by:

* POV;
* chronology;
* political context;
* cultural context;
* reader orientation after a substantial gap;
* or changed circumstances.

Do not delete unfamiliar terminology merely because it requires attention.

Do not add explanatory paragraphs simply because a fictional institution, technology, culture, species, religion, military formation, or title is unusual.

Provide only the information required for the scene and story.

---

### 24. SCENE-ENDING AUDIT

Inspect the final paragraphs of every scene.

Identify the strongest natural endpoint.

If the scene continues afterward only to:

* explain;
* summarize;
* interpret;
* moralize;
* repeat;
* preview the obvious;
* or soften the impact;

cut the tail.

Prefer ending on:

* an action;
* an image;
* a line of dialogue;
* a physical reaction;
* a decision;
* a discovery;
* a departure;
* a death;
* or silence.

Do not manufacture cliffhangers where none belong.

---

### 25. PARAGRAPH-ENDING AUDIT

Inspect weak final sentences within paragraphs.

Remove trailing explanations after stronger preceding sentences.

If a paragraph's strongest line occurs before its final sentence, determine whether the remaining material belongs:

* elsewhere;
* in another paragraph;
* rewritten;
* or deleted.

Do not routinely append interpretive commentary.

---

### 26. FORMAT AND TYPOGRAPHY AUDIT

Correct:

* malformed paragraph breaks;
* accidental merged dialogue;
* duplicate headings;
* inconsistent heading hierarchy;
* inconsistent capitalization;
* punctuation errors;
* quotation-mark errors;
* spacing defects;
* accidental page artifacts;
* and other formatting defects.

Preserve intentional formatting.

Do not introduce em dashes or en dashes where the manuscript style prohibits them.

Use punctuation consistent with the established house style.

---

### 27. CANON AND CAUSALITY CHECK

After all edits in the pass, verify that no change has:

* altered an established fact;
* broken chronology;
* removed necessary causality;
* altered a character's motivation;
* changed who performed an action;
* changed who knows information;
* changed an established relationship;
* changed genealogy;
* altered succession;
* removed necessary setup;
* removed necessary payoff;
* damaged worldbuilding;
* changed the result of a battle;
* changed a political consequence;
* created a geographical contradiction;
* created a technological contradiction;
* created a religious contradiction;
* or produced a new continuity error.

Repair any damage immediately.

Canon takes priority over stylistic simplification.

---

### 28. DEAD-STOP AUDIT

Silently search the entire revised manuscript again for:

* explanatory sentences after actions;
* explanatory sentences after emotional landings;
* unnecessary realization language;
* emotional translation after physical reactions;
* explanations of symbolism;
* explanations of dialogue;
* explanations after revelations;
* thematic restatement;
* redundant conclusions;
* repeated information;
* semantic repetition;
* lexical repetition;
* duplicated passages;
* duplicated excerpts;
* artificial prose;
* formulaic prose;
* conspicuously AI-like constructions;
* unnecessary verbosity;
* weak paragraph endings;
* scenes continuing after their strongest ending;
* unnecessary abstractions after concrete images;
* article and determiner errors;
* prohibited vocabulary;
* every occurrence of "memory";
* every occurrence of "pressure";
* every occurrence of prohibited "name" variants;
* and unnecessary uses of "own."

Do not report the remaining problems.

Correct them directly.

---

### 29. PASS COMPARISON

Compare the current manuscript against the manuscript at the beginning of the pass.

If substantive improvements were made:

UNCHANGED_PASSES = 0

If no substantive improvements were necessary:

UNCHANGED_PASSES = UNCHANGED_PASSES + 1

A substantive improvement includes correction of:

* a Dead-Stop violation;
* unnecessary repetition;
* an article error;
* prohibited vocabulary;
* artificial prose;
* a continuity defect;
* a duplicated passage;
* a meaningful formatting defect;
* an unnecessary abstraction;
* or another genuine prose defect.

Purely cosmetic synonym swapping is not substantive.

---

### 30. CONTINUE OR TERMINATE

If substantive violations remain and:

PASS < MAX_PASSES

then:

PASS = PASS + 1

Run the complete loop again using the newly revised manuscript as the input.

Terminate early if:

* the manuscript passes the substantive audits;
* another pass would produce only cosmetic changes;
* another pass would merely exchange one acceptable phrase for another;
* further simplification would flatten the voice;
* further deletion would damage atmosphere;
* further shortening would damage rhythm;
* further editing would remove necessary worldbuilding;
* or another pass risks altering canon, characterization, chronology, causality, or emotional force.

Do not continue merely to reach eight passes.

---

## FINAL MANUSCRIPT PASS

After the iterative loop terminates, read the complete manuscript one final time.

Confirm silently that:

1. The Dead-Stop Rule has been applied throughout.
2. Explanatory aftershocks have been removed.
3. Necessary story information remains.
4. Canon remains intact.
5. Causality remains intact.
6. Chronology remains intact.
7. Character motivations remain intact.
8. Character knowledge remains intact.
9. Established relationships remain intact.
10. Genealogy and succession remain intact.
11. Necessary worldbuilding remains intact.
12. POV remains consistent.
13. Strong imagery has not been flattened.
14. Emotional force remains.
15. Dialogue remains character-specific.
16. Artificial profundity has been removed.
17. Unnecessary abstractions have been simplified.
18. Actions are stated clearly.
19. Military results are understandable.
20. Political and dynastic relationships remain accurate.
21. Accidental repetition has been removed.
22. Deliberate motifs and callbacks remain.
23. Duplicate material has been removed.
24. Articles and determiners are grammatically correct.
25. Unnecessary uses of "own" have been removed.
26. Prohibited "name" constructions have been removed.
27. "memory" does not remain.
28. "pressure" does not remain.
29. Established project-specific prohibited vocabulary has been enforced.
30. Disguised metaphors have been removed where unnecessary.
31. Dialogue is not followed by redundant interpretation.
32. Physical reactions are not followed by redundant emotional translation.
33. Revelations are not followed by explanations of what the reader already understands.
34. Scene endings stop at their strongest appropriate point.
35. Paragraphs do not trail into unnecessary explanation.
36. Formulaic AI-style constructions have been removed.
37. Sentence rhythms remain varied and natural.
38. Formatting remains consistent.
39. No em dash or en dash has been introduced where prohibited.
40. No stylistic edit has created a continuity error.

If the final audit finds a substantive problem, correct it directly and recheck the affected scene plus any connected setup or payoff.

Do not reopen settled prose merely to make cosmetic changes.

---

## FINAL OUTPUT RULE

Return the fully edited manuscript itself.

Do not substitute:

* an editorial report;
* a critique;
* a summary;
* a list of edits;
* a list of recommendations;
* a diagnostic;
* a score;
* or an explanation of what should be changed.

Do not describe edits that can be made directly.

Make them.

If a revised manuscript file is requested, preserve the original file and create the revised version under the requested version number or filename unless explicitly instructed to overwrite the source.

The final manuscript must remain recognizably the author's work.

The objective is not to make the prose merely shorter.

The objective is to make it clearer, more concrete, less repetitive, less artificial, more natural, and stronger without changing the story.
