What Venezuelans say about life in Trinidad compared with Venezuela.

No comments have been collected. Every figure on this sheet is computed from a dataset that is currently empty, so every reading is zero and every percentage is withheld for want of a denominator. The scales below are drawn to the measurements the study will take. Nothing here is an estimate, a placeholder figure, or a projection.

      Missed about Venezuela

      Coded from expressions of loss, not from stated intention to return. Missing a country and planning to go back to it are different claims, and conflating them would overstate return intention — the error this study is most likely to make.

      Stay or leave

      A six-point scale, coded separately toward each country. Neutral and mixed are kept apart: neutral carries no evaluation, mixed carries two opposing ones, and collapsing them would lose exactly the ambivalence that matters most here.

      08

      Demographic comparison

      Five dimensions, coded only where a commenter states them. Nothing is inferred from a name, a photograph or a writing style. Any group below 30 distinct commenters is reported as a count and never as a percentage.

      09

      Voices

      Six to ten quotations, chosen to represent the leading themes rather than for how striking they are, and drawn only from first-hand comments coded at high confidence. Spanish appears in the original with an English translation beneath.

      10

      What this cannot tell you

      Social media comments are not a representative sample of Venezuelans in Trinidad and Tobago, and no amount of careful sampling makes them one. People without smartphones, without data, without written literacy in either language, and those avoiding visibility for reasons connected to their status are absent — and their absence is not random.

      Platform demographics tilt the picture again: TikTok runs younger, Facebook older. Algorithmic surfacing favours emotionally charged material over ordinary experience, so a comment section over-represents both the delighted and the furious.

      Findings from this instrument describe the public comments sampled. They are not a statement about all Venezuelans living in Trinidad and Tobago, and must not be written as one.
