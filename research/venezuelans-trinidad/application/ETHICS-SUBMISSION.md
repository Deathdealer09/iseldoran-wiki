## 1. Study

Analysis of public social media discourse in which Venezuelan migrants in
Trinidad and Tobago describe their circumstances there relative to Venezuela.
Target sample 5,000 public comments across TikTok, Facebook and Instagram,
obtained through the platforms' approved research programmes.

## 2. Human participants

The study analyses public statements. It does not recruit, contact, intervene
with, or collect data directly from any individual.

Individual consent is not sought, and the committee should scrutinise that
choice rather than accept it. The justification is that the material is
published publicly by its authors, that the analysis is of aggregate discourse
rather than of any person, that no individual is profiled, and that the
alternative — contacting migrants whose legal status may be irregular to seek
consent — would itself create the exposure risk the study is designed to avoid.

The committee should weigh against this that public posting is not the same as
consent to research use, and that the population may not anticipate their
comments being analysed. The mitigations in section 4 are offered on that basis,
not as a formality.

## 3. Vulnerability

The population is vulnerable in a concrete rather than a nominal sense. Some
members have irregular immigration status. Identification can lead to detention,
deportation, loss of employment, or exposure to people who exploit that
precarity. Several are separated from children.

This governs the design. Where a methodological gain conflicts with reducing
identification risk, the risk reduction wins, including where that means
reporting a weaker finding.

## 4. Risks and mitigations

**Re-identification through verbatim quotation.** A public comment is findable
by searching its text. Mitigations: quotation only from directly held material;
no quotation paired with attributes that narrow identity; categorical exclusion
of any quotation touching legal status, exploitation, or an identifiable
employer or address; a theme reported without a quotation where none can be
used safely.

**Re-identification through attribute combination.** Mitigations: suppression of
any demographic cell below 30 distinct commenters; no published cross-tab beyond
two dimensions; no publication of free-text location at finer than town level.

**Inference of legal status.** Mitigation: status coded only where the commenter
raises it, never inferred, and never used as a cross-tab dimension or a
reported category.

**Inference of protected characteristics.** Mitigation: gender, age and family
situation coded only from explicit self-statement. Never inferred from names,
photographs, writing style, accent or grammatical gender in another person's
reply. `unknown` is a permitted and expected value, and the pipeline treats it
as one.

**Hostile secondary use of findings.** Findings about migrants can be
weaponised. Mitigations: prominent statement of sampling limitations; explicit
refusal to characterise findings as representing all Venezuelans in Trinidad and
Tobago; equal prominence for favourable and unfavourable findings; no framing
that attributes a disposition to the population as a whole.

**Researcher-introduced bias.** Mitigations: collection separated from coding so
coders do not select material fitting a theme they hold in mind; a double-coded
subsample with disagreement reported; a pre-registered codebook; open coding
permitted so material is not forced into categories that misrepresent it.

## 5. Data protection

Author identifiers are replaced with salted one-way hashes at ingestion, before
analysis. The salt is stored separately under restricted permissions and never
travels with the data. No usernames, profile data, images, contact details or
location beyond region are collected. Full detail in the data management plan.

## 6. Benefit

Policy toward this population is made with limited evidence of how its members
describe their own circumstances, and the existing evidence is largely
agency-mediated, collected by organisations that may control access to services.
Unelicited discourse offers a differently biased view, and the divergence
between the two is informative for humanitarian programming and for public
understanding.

The benefit is modest and should not be overstated. It does not by itself
outweigh a serious risk of harm, which is why the mitigations above are designed
to keep that risk low rather than to justify accepting it.

## 7. Committee decisions sought

1. Approval to analyse public social media content without individual consent,
   on the reasoning in section 2.
2. Confirmation that the quotation exclusion rules in section 4 are sufficient,
   or direction to publish without quotations entirely.
3. Confirmation that the 30-commenter suppression threshold is appropriate for
   this population, or direction to set it higher.
4. Direction on retention periods for the coded dataset containing verbatim
   comment text.
