# Interlude 5 — The Courtier's Testimony (Excerpt)

What follows is excerpted verbatim from the sworn statement of Councillor Hadi Marun, taken under the authority of the Throne Court of Inquiry and entered into the formal prosecution record. I have abridged only where the testimony repeats matters established elsewhere in this account.

A. Khan, compiler

---

THRONE COURT OF INQUIRY

In the matter of the Bureau of Establishments and the Third Appointments Commission

Transcript of testimony given by Councillor Hadi Marun, under obligation, the deposing arbiter recording.

EXCERPT BEGINS.

ARBITER: State your office and your years of service.

MARUN: Councillor of the imperial court. Twenty-two years in service, eleven of them at the level of council. Before that I sat as a clerk to the Magistracy of the Inner Wards. My training is in administrative law. I say this so the court understands that I knew what the documents were when I touched them. I was not a man out of his depth. I want that on the record before anything else.

ARBITER: Describe how you were approached.

MARUN: There was no approach in the way the word suggests. No one came to my rooms in a hood. The first contact reached me by sealed correspondence, routed so that I could not trace its origin without effort I chose not to make. I understood from the wording that the sender knew my position, my debts, and the matter of the seat I had expected and had not received. The letter did not threaten me. It described a service and what the service would be worth. I read it twice. Then I answered it.

ARBITER: You answered in the affirmative.

MARUN: I did. I sealed my reply myself. No one held my hand to the wax. The court should not look for a moment when I was trapped, because there was none. There was a winter when I was tired and angry at a decision that had gone against me, a letter arrived that treated my anger as a reasonable thing, and I agreed to be useful. The smallness of it is the part I would change. It was a Tuesday. The seal smelled of the same lamp oil I had used for a decade.

ARBITER: Describe the service you provided.

MARUN: I authenticated documents. That was the whole of it, and it was enough. When an appointment had to be entered into the regional record under a carry-forward reconciliation, the entry needed a councillor's confirmation that the prior authority was sound. I gave that confirmation. I did not forge the appointments. I did not move the money, and at the time I did not know there was money to move. I confirmed that paper was in order when I had reason to believe it was not, and my confirmation made it real to every clerk downstream who trusted my office. A false thing becomes true when a man of standing signs beneath it. That was my function. I was the standing.

ARBITER: Name the contacts in the network known to you.

MARUN: I will name what I know and mark plainly what I only inferred, because the court deserves the difference. I knew a courier by the surname Beye. Rashid Beye. He carried sealed material between the regional offices and the points where it needed to be. I met him twice and would not have recognized him a third time; he was built to be forgotten. I knew of a deputy registrar at the Adwa office, Mokoena, by the pattern of his entries before I ever knew his name. We did not correspond. We were two hands of the same body that never touched.

ARBITER: And above them.

MARUN: Above them I inferred a single authority. I never had a name from any letter. But the authorizations that came down to me carried a category of seal that only a marshal of the Bureau could command, and the reach of the corrections crossed jurisdictions no lesser office could touch. A man in my work learns to read a hand by its weight. I knew the weight of that one. I will not pretend before this court that I was surprised when I learned whose it was.

ARBITER: When did you understand the full scope of what you served?

MARUN: Late. Later than I should admit. I told myself for years that I was confirming paper, that the harm was abstract, that the prefectures would absorb a few crooked magistrates as they had absorbed worse. I did not let myself add the entries together, because the sum was a thing I did not want to hold. I understood the scope on the day I was asked to authenticate the challenge against the investigation itself. That was the first time the work asked me to defend it rather than feed it. I authenticated the challenge. I want that recorded too. I did the wrong thing even after I understood, and then I sat in my rooms and waited to be found, which is its own kind of confession and not a brave one.

ARBITER: Is there anything you wish to add for the record.

MARUN: Only this. I was offered a different path once, years ago, by a colleague who is honest and never knew what I had become. I think of her. I am not asking the court for mercy and I am not asking it to understand me. I committed these acts as a competent man who knew the law, and I will answer for them as one. Let the record show that I did not weep and I did not blame the letter.

EXCERPT ENDS.

Sworn and recorded under the seal of the Throne Court of Inquiry, the eleventh day of the second imperial quarter. Filed to the prosecution record under judicial seal reference TCI-04-0117.
