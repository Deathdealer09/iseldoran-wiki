# AMIRA'S RULE — STORY BIBLE
### Continuity reference for the Amira's Rule manuscript (Iseldoran Sagas)

This document locks the invented names, places, and mechanisms that the production
briefs deliberately leave open, so that every section stays consistent. It is a
development reference, not part of the manuscript. Anything not fixed here should be
kept minimal and consistent with established Iseldoran Sagas canon.

---

## NAMING AND DEMOGRAPHIC STANDARD
The imperial demographic baseline is African and Afro-Mongolian. Names across the
court, the administration, and the Lionesses draw on African, Arabian, Mongolian, and
imperial-European roots, blended without comment. No character's appearance is treated
as remarkable for reflecting that baseline.

## PRINCIPAL CHARACTERS (canonical)
- **Amira** — Empress of the Trinitarian Throne. Governs rather than reigns. Composed,
  exact, unafraid of the work of ruling. Not warm toward Eleanor; a colleague of the
  highest order.
- **Eleanor De Grosvenor** — elderly, formidable, emotionally controlled. Head of the
  De Grosvenor intelligence and administrative apparatus, built over six decades.
  Biological mother of Kaviel. Governs through obligation, administration,
  intelligence, loyalty, and competence. Signs her private correspondence
  **"E. De Grosvenor"** — always that form, no flourish.
- **Kaviel De Grosvenor** — Eleanor's biological daughter. Senior Lioness, operative
  rather than courtier. Works from documents and inference where Eleanor works from
  networks. Carries the De Grosvenor name without performing it. Careful, professional
  relationship with Amira.
- **Asha** — canonical Lioness. Runs intake and assessment; precise, demanding,
  practical. Governed by standards, not personal loyalty. (Distinct from the historical
  God-Empress Asha Kers I; "Asha" is a common imperial name.)
- **Meret** — canonical Lioness. Disciplined, conscientious; does not ask questions when
  a superior requests files or material. Patient to the point of holding information
  until she is sure of it.
- **Silva** — canonical Lioness. Exceptional field observer; works peripheries and
  surveillance.
- **Captain Adulfus Khan** — compiler and historical narrator of the record. Veteran
  military officer, father of the line descended from Harlow Khan. Writes with a
  soldier's discipline, by stylus, at a physical desk in the Imperial Record Office.

## SUPPORTING CHARACTERS (invented — LOCKED)
- **Sefu Adebayo** — Eleanor's longest-serving trusted subordinate administrator.
  Appears in Chapter 3. Quiet, exact, loyal without sentiment. Eleanor addresses him as
  "Sefu." Recurring.
- **The courier** — full name **Rashid Beye**. A face in the crowd at Sabra Crossing in
  Chapter 4 (NOT named there). Moves false documentation between jurisdictions for the
  faction's network. In Chapter 8 the Lionesses arrive only at his surname **initial,
  "B."** He is not named in full until later in the manuscript (Act Two). A middle
  figure, not a principal.
- **The protected individual** — internal reference name **Naila Osei** (a witness moved
  for her own safety). UNNAMED in Chapter 4 and throughout Act One; referred to only by
  role.
- **Councillor Hadi Marun** — a courtier introduced in Chapter 7 as established and
  outwardly sympathetic. He is a person in a difficult position, not a villain. He
  commits to the faction later (Act Two). In Act One he is merely present and likable.
- **Other Chapter 7 courtiers (minor):** **Lady Provost Imara Shandt**, **Magister Faraj
  Oduya**, **Councillor Otho Bey**. The unremarkable antagonist-adjacent figure in
  Chapter 7 stays UNNAMED and forgettable.
- **The source Eleanor protects** — internal reference **Senior Magister Aurelio Saad**,
  a senior administrative figure who has fed the De Grosvenor apparatus for twenty-one
  years through indirect channels. UNNAMED in Act One; referenced only as "the source."
- **The junior Lioness researcher** — internal reference **Zola Adimu**. Does not appear
  in Act One; unnamed until later in the manuscript.

## PLACES (LOCKED)
- **The Imperial Record Office** — on New Terra, the administrative seat. Stone walls,
  rows of catalogued testimony, the smell of pressed document wax. Adulfus Khan works
  here. (Prologue, Epilogue.)
- **The Trinitarian Throne Room** — Amira's court on New Terra. High ceilings, marble
  floors with old repair lines showing through the polish, incense, cold stone, high
  windows that cut angled light across the floor.
- **The Lioness Quarter / compound** — the Lionesses' training and administrative
  compound in the imperial district. Utilitarian, continuously running. Small assessment
  rooms built so sound does not travel.
- **Eleanor's private office suite** — within the imperial administrative wing. Old
  furniture chosen for function, a west-facing window over an interior courtyard she
  never uses, maps on the walls, aged paper, cold tea.
- **Amira's small reception room** — adjacent to her private quarters, off the record.
  Two chairs, a low table, a window over an interior garden neither she nor Eleanor
  looks at. Site of the three private Amira/Eleanor meetings.
- **Sabra Crossing** — a transit waystation on the imperial periphery. Functional,
  crowded, the kind of place where important things move without appearing important.
  Engine exhaust, vendor food, artificial light built for continuous movement. (Chapter
  4; the manifest recurs in Chapter 6.)
- **Kaviel's administrative office** — within the Lioness compound. A single lamp, a
  window she cracks for moving air.

## THE APPOINTMENTS FRAUD (mechanism — LOCKED)
- **Surface:** false judicial and administrative appointments inserted into regional
  imperial records, made to look routinely confirmed.
- **The instrument:** the **Third Appointments Commission**, a routine body convened to
  review and confirm judicial and administrative appointments across the **Outer
  Prefectures**, seated under the **Bureau of Establishments**. Its minutes contain a
  provision called the **"carry-forward reconciliation"** — a clause permitting
  appointments confirmed under a prior commission's authority to be entered into the
  current record without fresh review, to reconcile backlog. The faction uses this
  clause to backdate fraudulent appointments under cover of legitimate reconciliation.
  It reads as ordinary bureaucracy.
- **Chapter 1 seed:** a petitioner contests a regional magistrate's ruling out of the
  **Sennar Prefecture**. In the procedural detail, the magistrate's appointment is shown
  as confirmed by the Third Appointments Commission under a carry-forward entry — but
  the confirmation date precedes the commission's session. The dates cannot both be
  true. Amira and the reader pass over it. Eleanor catches it.
- **Region most touched early:** the **Sennar Prefecture** (one of the Outer
  Prefectures). The scope later proves far wider.

## THE HELD REPORT (Chapter 3 to Chapter 6 — LOCKED)
- A field report out of a peripheral jurisdiction flagging an unusual cluster of
  appointment confirmations that all trace back to carry-forward entries. Eleanor holds
  it eleven days to protect its source (Aurelio Saad). She releases it in Chapter 6.
- **The three files Kaviel pulls in Chapter 6** (without being told to):
  1. the Sennar Prefecture magistrate record (the matter from Chapter 1),
  2. a transit manifest from Sabra Crossing (the movement Kaviel ran in Chapter 4),
  3. a prior appointments-commission roster (to test the carry-forward chain).
  By the end she has a shape, no name. She writes one word, folds it, pockets it.

## THE INTELLIGENCE THREAD (Chapter 8 — LOCKED)
- Meret has watched a pattern for six weeks: the same low-level figure recurring at
  transfer points across implicated jurisdictions. She brings it to Asha. They reduce it
  to a surname **initial, "B."** — the courier, Rashid Beye, the same face Kaviel saw at
  Sabra Crossing. Asha logs the initial and resolves to work from what they have.

## STYLE GUARDRAILS (hard rules)
- No em dashes. No en dashes. Use commas, periods, semicolons, or restructure.
- No "Not X but Y" constructions.
- No chapter or scene endings that resolve into a thematic summary or aphorism.
- No three-part rhythmic lists (e.g. "cold, dark, and silent").
- Banned words: simply, entirely, actually, clearly, genuinely, particularly, specific,
  specifically, particular, manner, continuity, inevitable, inevitability, sequence.
- At least three senses integrated naturally per chapter, never stacked.
- No combat. The conflict is administrative, relational, and political.
- Interludes are documents/letters, not prose scenes. Keep the format.
- Eleanor is earned, never warm. The Lionesses are professional, never theatrical.
- Characters do not explain to each other what they both already know, and do not
  narrate their own emotions.
