# Interlude 3 — Silva: Field Notes, Jurisdiction Seven

These pages were carried out of Jurisdiction Seven in the lining of a freight ledger and held in the Lioness compound for some weeks before anyone read them as evidence. I reproduce them as written, in the field shorthand of their author, because the line that mattered was set down beside lines that did not, and I would have the reader find it where she found it.

---

J7. FIELD LOG. Posting under cover of freight audit, Meridian feeder spur. Three weeks. S.

DAY 1. Arrived ahead of schedule, took the spur clerk's room over the weighbridge. Smell of axle grease through the floorboards day and night. Vantage on the lower yard, the attestation shed, the courier gate. Established as auditor's runner. No one asks a runner anything.

DAY 2. Mapped the shed traffic. Couriers in at the half-hour, satchels chopped, out the south gate. Nothing here a manifest would not hold. Pattern is the point. Watch the breaks in the pattern.

DAY 4. Mokoena's man through the gate, southern feeder. Recognized from Meridian. Beye. Carried the attestation satchel, left it at the shed window, did not wait for the chop. A courier who does not wait for his chop is not carrying his own paper. Noted. Said nothing.

DAY 6. Shed clerk drinks at the weighbridge canteen, talks. Says the J7 reconciliation went up clean this quarter, fewer holds than Sennar, faster. Says it the way a man says a thing he was told to be pleased about. The bread here is the flat gray kind, sour, and the tea is worse, and a clerk who buys both for a stranger wants the stranger to stay and listen.

DAY 9. Quiet. Logged the gate counts. Sat the yard from the cold side so the sun would not put me on the glass.

DAY 11. Black transit, no facility chop, in through the freight gate after the day shed had closed. This does not happen on a spur. A man came down from it. Heavy build, deliberate, an aide a half-step behind carrying nothing, which is how you know the man matters. He did not go to the shed. He went to the spur registrar's office, which was dark, and the registrar opened it for him, and the lamp came up behind the blind. Forty minutes. The aide stood at the door the whole forty and watched the yard and saw a runner crossing it with a folio case and did not mark her, because a runner with a folio case at that hour is the yard working as the yard works.

DAY 11, later. Did not log the man's face to the registry. Logged it here. I have seen it. I will set down where. The Bureau circular on attestation forms carried a plate of the New Terra office at its standardization session, the senior table, and his face is on the plate, third from the convening chair. A man whose face is on the plate of the New Terra senior table does not come down a freight spur after dark to sit forty minutes in a dark registrar's office and leave no chop, no entry a ledger could hold. He came to be in the room. Whatever was done in the forty minutes was done by his being in the room, the way Mokoena's entries were done by a true name carrying a false errand. I do not yet know that this means what I think it means. I am not going to write it up until I do.

DAY 12. The spur registrar burned something in the canteen stove before first light. Sour smoke, paper. I was on the cold side and saw the flue take it. A registrar who burns at his own stove and not at the public incinerator on the terrace is burning a thing he does not want carried past his own door.

DAY 14. Beye through again. Same satchel routine. The reconciliation schedule he leaves is thicker by a third than the J7 traffic warrants. The traffic does not warrant it. Something is being moved into J7 that did not originate in J7. Noted. Did not act.

DAY 17. Confirmed the black transit's registry plate against the freight gate's own counter log, which counts axles and keeps no names. One uncharted entry, Day 11, after close. The gate counter does not lie and does not know what it counted.

DAY 19. The aide was at the canteen at midday, alone, no transit in the yard. So he has been left here. A man who matters does not leave his own aide on a freight spur unless the spur is now part of the work and wants a steady hand on it. The work has put down a root here. I have what the records cannot give. I am carrying it out by hand.

DAY 21. Closed the posting. Returned the clerk's room as found. Freight audit filed clean, no irregularity, signed off by the spur registrar himself, who shook my hand at the gate and was glad to see the auditor's runner go.

Folio reference: J7 freight audit, supplementary. Filed routine. Day 21, third quarter.

S.
