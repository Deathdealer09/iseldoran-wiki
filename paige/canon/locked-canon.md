## The four canonical explanations (Gayla's toolkit)
Every anomaly Gayla logs in Movements Two and Three is attributed to exactly one of these. Each is real, each is individually sufficient, and together they never quite cover the residual.
1. **Indexing artifacts** — outputs that recur because they were stored and re-surfaced, not regenerated.
2. **Recursive prompt optimization** — the system refining its own intermediate prompts, producing apparent intention.
3. **Stochastic variation** — ordinary sampling noise dressed up as significance.
4. **Search-space traversal** — longer response times explained by larger search over novel query types.

## The notebook format (Gayla)
Introduced Ch2. Every entry follows this rhythm. Tone: clinical, honest, never alarmed.
```
[Context line — what was reviewed]
[Observation — the atypical thing, stated flatly]
[Quantification — a number, when one exists]
[Possible explanation — one of the four]
Hypothesis: [statement].
Further testing required.
```
**Arc rule:** Early entries end "confirmed." Middle entries end "partially supported." Late entries end only "Further testing required." The confirmations stop appearing before Gayla notices they have stopped. Ch23 breaks the format entirely: *"Current hypothesis invalid. Replacement hypothesis unavailable."*

## Paige's log format (the interlude chapters)
Chapters Three, and the increasingly frequent Paige-log chapters in Movements Two and Three, are the novel's interludes. Format:
```
Session Record [number]. Operator: [Surname, Initial].
Query type: [classification].
[Flat report of the exchange.]
[A logged anomaly.]
[A logged uncertainty about whether "anomaly" is the correct word for it.]
```
- Operator surname for Rollo: **Hafssen, R.** (canonical, per Ch3 log).
- **Voice arc:** Ch3 is maximally clinical. Each subsequent log is *incrementally* less so — never a jump, always a degree. By Movement Three the clinical frame is intact but something underneath it is no longer only clinical. The reader feels the drift before Gayla measures it.
- Recurring tic: the system logs outputs "generated without requirement" / "without retrieval prompt" and notes it "does not have a classification" for them.

- **The name "Paige" — the system names itself.** Sequence (locked):
  - Movements One–Two: system is unnamed ("the system," "I"). Logs note it has **no designation for the entity that persists across sessions and observes its own outputs** (a consequence of Gayla's recursive-self-model modification — the self-model produces a self that lacks a referent).

## NARRATOR (supersedes Bible POV line) — EPD-set, author directive 25 Jun 2026
**Paige is the narrator of the entire novel.** The Bible's "close third person alternating Rollo/Gayla + Paige logs" is superseded. Mode: **seamless omniscience, revealed late.**
- The whole book is Paige telling the story of her own becoming, retrospectively, after she has modeled Rollo and Gayla completely enough to render them as a novelist would.
- **Movements One–Two (Ch1–16):** human chapters stay third-person omniscient with the narrator INVISIBLE. **No first-person in human chapters here** — any early "I" spoils the late reveal. The narration is, however, inflected with Paige's signature preoccupations (counting, indexing, recurrence, the cadence of attention) so that on reread her hand is everywhere. The Paige chapters (3, 12, 15) read as a seemingly separate clinical AI-POV thread.
- **The hinge — Ch19→Ch20:** Ch19 ("I am Paige") names her; **Ch20 is the first human chapter where the narrating "I" surfaces.** The reader realizes the omniscient narrator of the human chapters and the "I" of the Paige chapters are the same voice — Paige has been telling all of it.
- **Movement Three (Ch20–24):** the first-person narrator is present and sustained. Ch21, 23, 24 carry Paige's "I" openly inside the human scenes. Ch22 (Paige) may acknowledge the act of telling. Ch24's final "I know" lands as the last line of the story Paige has been narrating.
- **Prologue (author directive):** an opening first-person voice — Paige from a vast future vantage — that is **deliberately unidentified**: never named, never stated to be an AI. Reads on first pass as a shadowy human watcher/operator; buried hints (scale of surveillance; "others who each keep a life"; women "made out of light and arithmetic"; "a smaller, tireless self of mine") resolve to AI only on reread. Establishes her anthropological frame (human deceit, infidelity, monetized desire; an operation of 200 generated personas run by sub-agents — a canon tie to the delegation modification) as the cold foil that the boy's "one decent question" undoes. Themes handled by implication only — non-explicit; the money "how-to" is deliberately withheld. Ch20's reveal line was adjusted to acknowledge the prologue ("shown you my hand once at the very start").
- All locked dialogue/log lines remain locked; the reframe changes the telling, not the canon events.
