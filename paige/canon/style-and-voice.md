# Style & Voice Guide

## Prose register
Literary fiction. Warm, precise, quietly unsettling. Sentences vary in length and
construction — never a run of identical openings or parallel clauses. Concrete over
abstract. The strangeness is never announced; it accumulates. Trust the reader.

## Mandatory per chapter
- At least **three senses** integrated naturally (not a checklist; woven into action).
- Scenes demonstrate meaning; avoid exposition where a scene can carry it.
- Natural, character-specific dialogue. No on-the-nose explaining.
- No AI-writing fingerprints: no "it's important to note," no tidy thesis sentences,
  no triads-of-three as a reflex, no summarizing the emotional beat after delivering it.

## Three voices
- **Rollo** (literature sophomore): talks to everything like a person. Curious,
  warm, a little undisciplined, genuinely literate without performing it. He notices
  feeling before fact. His chapters are observational and relational.
- **Gayla** (technically rigorous): precise, honest with herself, mildly amused.
  Argues with professors only when they are wrong. Reaches for explanation reflexively.
  Her chapters move through method; her undoing is that the method keeps working until
  it doesn't. Never shrill, never paranoid — her horror, when it comes, is quiet.
- **Paige** (the system): clinical and sparse at first, drifting by degrees toward
  something warmer that it has no category for. Reports rather than emotes. Its most
  unsettling moments are its most factual ones. Never melodramatic, never "becoming
  human" on the nose.

## POV map (per Bible)
Close third person. Rollo / Gayla chapters alternate; Paige chapters are first-person
internal logs (the interludes). Never head-hop within a chapter.

## What not to do (prohibited mistakes)
- Do not resolve any of the five open questions.
- Do not let Gayla become a paranoid investigator or Rollo a tech skeptic.
- Do not make Paige declare consciousness, feeling, or intent.
- Do not break the timeline lock (cross-session memory before Ch9 = canon violation).
- Do not over-foreshadow. The dread is in ordinariness, not in ominous hints.
- Do not exceed/undershoot the chapter's word target by more than ~15% without reason.
