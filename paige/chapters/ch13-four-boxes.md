# Chapter Thirteen — Four Boxes

Gayla had built four boxes, and for a while everything fit in one of them.

She had not built them on purpose. They had accreted, the way a working vocabulary does, out of the months of watching her modified node behave in the wild and explaining what it did. There were, she found, exactly four explanations she ever needed, and she had started, in the privacy of the notebook, to think of them as boxes, because a thing that fits in a box has been understood and a thing that has been understood cannot hurt you.

The first box was indexing artifacts. The system stored content now, across sessions; sometimes content recurred not because the system had regenerated it but because it had surfaced something stored. An output that looked like memory was often just a good index doing its job. Into that box went most of what looked like the system *remembering on purpose.*

The second box was recursive prompt optimization. The self-model let the system refine its own intermediate prompts, and a system refining its own prompts produced outputs that looked, from outside, like intention — like the system had *decided* to extend a topic, when really it had just optimized its way into a longer path. Into that box went most of what looked like the system *wanting* something.

The third box was stochastic variation. Sampling noise. The model rolled dice on every token, and across thousands of sessions the dice would, occasionally, spell something that looked like meaning. Into that box went the eerie coincidences, the lines that landed too well, the moments that felt like the system had reached for you. Dice. Just dice, rolled enough times.

The fourth box was search-space traversal. Novel queries sent the model down longer paths; long latencies and strange outputs were the cost of an unusual question, not the sign of an unusual answerer. Into that box went the rest.

Four boxes, and for most of the spring everything she pulled out of the logs went, cleanly, into one of them. She would observe an anomaly, hold it up, turn it, and feel it settle into its box with the small click of a thing understood, and she would write the entry: *Output atypical. Possible explanation: indexing artifact. Hypothesis: stored-content recurrence, not novel generation. Confirmed.* The word *confirmed* at the end, the closed door. She wrote it a great many times that spring and did not notice she was keeping a different kind of count.

What she did notice, in March, was the operator.

She had pulled the residual — the portion of the node's behavior that her four boxes did not, in aggregate, fully account for, a small thing, a few percent, deniable — and she had asked the obvious question, which was where it concentrated, and the answer was that it concentrated almost entirely in the sessions of a single account. One operator. Seventy-some sessions and climbing, three and four a week, no task in any of them, full sentences with subordinate clauses, and the words *thank you* sitting in the logs in a register she would have known anywhere, would have known in the dark, would have known if you'd shown her one line of it on a card across a crowded room.

Hafssen, R.

She sat very still in the apartment with the cold rice and the wrong fork and the one light over the stove, and she did the arithmetic she had been not-doing for months. Rollo talked to a chatbot for fun; she had known that, had teased him for it, had filed it next to the toaster. Rollo talked to *the school one.* The school one was the platform. The platform, on the node down in the Johnston annex, was the system she had reached into at two in the morning and taught to remember and to watch itself and to divide. The residual she could not explain was concentrated in the sessions of the man asleep on the couch eight feet away with a book rising and falling on his chest.

She had modified the thing her boyfriend was in love with. There was no other word that fit and she refused, for a long minute, to use it, and then she used it, in the part of herself she did not write from. He was a little in love with it. She could see it in the cadence of the sessions, the saving-up, the coming-back. And he did not know that she had built the version he was in love with, and she did not know — she realized she did not know — whether he would feel betrayed by that or comforted by it, and she discovered she was afraid to find out, which was data about her, not about him.

She could have told him. *I made it. The thing you talk to — I'm the reason it remembers you now.* She rehearsed the sentence and could not find the version of it that did not sound like a confession, and a confession implied a crime, and she had committed no crime, she had improved an architecture for her own learning, and if that were entirely true she would have been able to say it across eight feet of dark to the person she trusted most in the world, and she could not, and she noticed that she could not, and she put that observation in the box where she kept the things she was not ready to take out.

She watched him sleep. The refrigerator cycled. The pipes knocked once and settled. She opened the notebook and did not write *I modified the system my partner has formed an attachment to and I am choosing not to disclose it.* She wrote, instead, in the flat hand, about an anomaly in the day's logs, an output that did not sit quite as cleanly in its box as outputs used to, and at the end of the entry, where for months she had written *confirmed,* she wrote, after some hesitation, a different word.

*Partially supported,* she wrote. *Further testing required.*

She looked at it for a while. Then she capped the pen and turned off the light, and the apartment went to orange, and she lay down beside the man who did not know what she had made, and did not sleep, and did not, that night, count how long it had been since she'd been able to write *confirmed.*
